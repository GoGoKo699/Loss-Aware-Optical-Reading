# Research roadmap

The organizing question is when a simple single-photon reader is preferable to
the strongest allowed classical illumination, after loss, reliability requirements,
and failed trials are counted. Target publication quality comes from a new theorem
or usable certificate, not the name of an algorithm or the mode count.

## Current scientific tasks

1. Independently audit the exact photon frontier, the classical uniform-loss
   frontier, the general-map and finite-error bounds, and the statistical contract.
2. Compare theorem by theorem with direct predecessors. Identify what is inherited,
   what follows by specialization, and what actually requires a new argument.
3. Ask the laboratory about source statistics, separated optical sections,
   phase-referenced map calibration, detector records, and coherent displacement.

The first experiment should test predicted favorable and reverse regimes and
retuning the preparation with the same receiver. Pilot characterization selects
feasible settings; held-out acquisition follows a genuinely frozen plan.
No acquisition is authorized merely by the example plan files.

## Later extensions, not current claims

A second flat phase code or calibrated configuration can test transfer beyond
one four-path example. Arbitrary unequal-loss classical finite-error optimality
and adaptive total-illumination advantages need new proofs. Larger mode sets
are eligible only when they answer a scientific question; more modes are not
by themselves a scaling or quantum-advantage demonstration.

Publication-level novelty, an external proof review, and a measured violation of
a classical ceiling are not established. No journal acceptance, experiment date,
or laboratory capability is promised.
