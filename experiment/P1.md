# P1: photon performance above the classical ceiling

**Question:** does the unconditional score of the photon reader exceed the
derived bound for every permitted classical illumination and receiver under
the same declared incident signal budget? Use the available SU(4) chip or four
active modes of the available SU(8) chip. This is the registered positive test;
its meaning and statistics remain those in
[FIRST_EXPERIMENT.md](FIRST_EXPERIMENT.md).

Begin with [commissioning](COMMISSIONING.md) and [M1](M1.md). A source-class
certificate additionally needs calibrated source/mean/tail bounds, common-phase
device maps with simultaneous coverage, a blinded independent hidden section,
complete trial/readout accounting and a fixed preselected acquisition plan.
These are unsupplied laboratory inputs, not missing processors.

## What to program

Use columns for amplitude vectors and output rows/input columns for matrices.
Logical ports and labels are `0,1,2,3`. A photon at P0 has
`a=(sqrt(p_0),...,sqrt(p_3))`; the hidden section between P0 and P1 applies

$$
O_j=I_4-2|j\rangle\langle j|,\qquad
T=\mathrm{diag}(\sqrt{\eta_0},\ldots,\sqrt{\eta_3}),
$$

with fresh independent uniform `j`. The receiver after P1 is fixed at
`D4=J4/2-I4`. For `lambda=5`, compute `p_i=z_i^2` from the positive
normalized top eigenvector of

$$
B_5=\frac64vv^T-5\,\mathrm{diag}(\eta),\qquad v_i=\sqrt{\eta_i}.
$$

The illustrative target `eta=(.7,.7,.7,.56)` gives
`p≈(.24058994,.24058994,.24058994,.27823018)`. Source vacuum/multiphoton
contamination is a separate P0 model; it is not removed by renormalizing this
input vector or by conditioning on a click.

For SU8 use ports 0–3, amplitudes `a ⊕ 0`, hidden matrices `O_j ⊕ I4`,
loss `T ⊕ I4` and receiver `D4 ⊕ I4`; inputs 4–7 are vacuum. Any spare
output click is failure and remains in the eight-bit record. The exact matrices,
preparation unitary, phase convention, and physical roles are on
[SU4](SU4.md), [SU8 route A](SU8.md#route-a-four-active-modes), and
[interfaces](INTERFACES.md). T requires physical attenuation. An answer-dependent
compiled end-to-end matrix is only a known transfer check.

## What to freeze and record

1. Complete the [uncertainty worksheet](UNCERTAINTY.md) using separate
   calibration/pilot records. Bound signal mean and high-number tail energy at
   P0; calibrate the maps from P0 to P1 in a shared optical phase convention.
   Do not absorb downstream receiver/detector loss into the competitor's device.
2. Choose preparation from that pretest calibration. Freeze receiver, output
   decision, penalty, N, calibration/source envelopes, condition IDs and code
   hashes before the held-out run. Calibration can realize the target before
   acquisition; no held-out tuning is permitted.
3. Retain `lambda=5` unless the pilot supplies the protocol's pretest reason
   for another value. The provisional template has **N=500,000** and
   `alpha_stat=.00025`. A revised N is frozen after the pilot, before held-out
   outcomes; the example is not an automatically approved lab plan.
4. Draw each hidden label independently and uniformly for each single-use
   attempt, isolate it from source/reader control, allow settling, and use a
   label-independent fixed exposure window. A pre-exposure clock/herald defines
   the attempt. Acquire the fixed N, seal optical records, then unseal labels.
5. Count a single active click as its port label. Count no click, multiple click,
   leakage and predeclared rejected exposed records as inconclusive. Preserve
   every raw row and every mask; do not stop on first significance or remove
   unfavorable attempts. A whole-run abort uses the predeclared rule.

Required raw fields are trial ID, condition/calibration/phase-code/receiver/
preparation IDs, sealed-then-unsealed label, full click mask, pre-exposure herald
or clock reference, gate times, energy-bound ID and integrity flags. Retain
the raw detector/time-tag evidence supporting them. SU4 has 16 masks; the SU8
embedding preserves 256 before its explicit four-label adaptation.

## Which comparison supports the claim

Each attempt scores `1` for correct, `-5` for wrong and `0` for inconclusive.
For a calibrated classical contrast upper bound `kappa_U` and incident mean
upper bound `mu_U`, the four-label classical score ceiling is

$$
U_{\rm cl}=\min\{1,\tfrac{15}{14}[1-e^{-\kappa_U\mu_U}]\}.
$$

The fixed-N statistical radius is

$$
r_N=6\sqrt{\frac{\log(1/\alpha_{\rm stat})}{2N}}.
$$

Compare `observed (C-5E) - r_N`, after any predeclared source/model score
subtraction, with `U_cl`. Use the actual phase-referenced maps and valid
uncertainty bounds, not a fit to a weak-coherent control. The competitor includes
arbitrary allowed receivers, mixtures with receiver-known preparation labels,
phase references and rare bright pulses at the declared mean signal budget.
The general unequal-loss ceiling is a bound, not a universally attained frontier.

The preserved program-wide allocation is at most `.0005` for simultaneous
source/energy/channel calibration failures, `.00025` for P1 statistics and
`.00025` for P2 statistics: combined error at most `.001` under the declared
assumptions. Secondary comparisons require descriptive intervals or a separate
multiplicity plan. There is no optional-stopping claim.

## Interpretation and failure modes

A positive valid certificate excludes the declared classical source class
under its resource and calibration assumptions. It does not demonstrate
device independence, a total-energy or speed advantage, or performance of
an arbitrary quantum architecture. A negative or inconclusive result does
not prove classical superiority.

Unbounded tail energy inferred only from finite click data, postselection on
survival, hidden-label leakage, independent phase gauges for fitted maps,
unmodeled accessible output modes, or test-driven receiver changes can invalidate
the interpretation even if the program prints a positive gap. Calibration
coverage is an external input, not something the analysis software proves.
See the [theory route](../docs/THEORY_ROUTE.md),
[Theorems 3–4 and finite-N proof](../proofs/THEORY.md),
[numerical contract](../docs/NUMERICAL_CONTRACT.md),
[robustness guide](../docs/ROBUSTNESS_GUIDE.md), and
[uncertainty worksheet](UNCERTAINTY.md).
